# MoonDeck — the pitch · Narrative & presenter notes

The story this deck tells, slide by slide. This deck pitches MoonDeck itself — to MoonPay —
and was built in MoonDeck, which is half the argument. Keep the deck honest to this spine.

## Mission
**Audience:** MoonPay — leadership and anyone who makes decks.
**The decision to drive:** adopt MoonDeck across the org — install the app instead of buying
seats of a browser-based AI deck tool.

## The thesis (one sentence)
You already did the work — it's scattered across Slack, Drive, Linear, Notion, and your laptop —
and MoonDeck turns it into the finished presentation on your machine, with the AI you already
pay for, at ~$0 marginal cost.

## The arc
Open on the pain everyone recognizes: the work is done, but it's scattered, and the deck is an
evening of re-packaging — pure tax. Show the answer: a Mac app you just talk to, slides building
live. Then the two reasons only this shape works: it runs where your work lives (a browser tab
can't see your files or your tools), and a presentation is just HTML — the one thing an LLM
writes natively, which also gives decks everything code gets. Prove it with real keynotes and a
real reaction. Then the money: "Gamma, but for MoonPay" at ~$0 marginal cost, ~1.8 person-years
a year back. Land on the bigger return — ideas stop dying in docs — and close the loop: install it.

## Slide-by-slide

### 1 — MoonDeck · *Cover*
**Idea:** Turn the work you've already done into the presentation.
**On screen:** MoonDeck wordmark · "Turn the work you've already done into a beautiful presentation."
**Say:** "This deck is about MoonDeck — and was built by MoonDeck. Everything you'll see, it made."
**Hands off:** start with the evening everyone here has lost →

### 2 — The deck tax · *Problem*
**Idea:** The work is already done — it's just scattered across your tools.
**On screen:** four logo cards — Slack (the decisions) · Google Drive (the numbers) · Linear (the
roadmap) · Notion (the doc) → arrow → "a deck"; punch: an evening re-packaging what you already knew.
**Say:** "You've already done the work. The decisions are in Slack, the numbers in a Sheet, the
roadmap in Linear, the doc in Notion, the code on your laptop. Then someone needs a presentation —
and you lose an evening copying it out of ten tabs."
**Hands off:** and that tax isn't small →

### 3 — Hours, quantified · *Problem (cost)*
**Idea:** 2–6 hours a deck; ~100 hours a year for people who present often.
**On screen:** two KPI figures — "2–6 hrs · one polished deck, by hand" · "~100 hrs · a year, presenting often."
**Say:** "A polished deck is a 2–6 hour job. Present monthly and that's roughly 100 hours a year.
The thinking was done hours ago — the deck is pure tax."
**Hands off:** here's what we built instead →

### 4 — Say it, watch it build · *What we do*
**Idea:** A Mac app you talk to; the slides build live beside the chat.
**On screen:** feature split — "Say it. Watch the deck build." beside the live app capture.
**Say:** "MoonDeck is a Mac app. You say 'make a deck on the Q2 launch' or 'turn this Linear
project into a board update' — and the slides build live next to the chat, themed and on-brand."
**Hands off:** why can it do what a website can't? →

### 5 — It can reach your work · *Why only this works*
**Idea:** It runs on your machine, on Claude/Codex — so it can actually reach your work.
**On screen:** two cards — your machine (the files on your laptop) · your tools (Slack · Drive ·
Linear · Notion logos); Claude + Codex marks; punch: a browser tab can't see any of that.
**Say:** "It runs on your computer, on the coding agents you already use — Claude or Codex. Those
agents read the files on your machine and pull from your connected tools. A browser-based deck
tool can't see any of that. An app on your machine can."
**Hands off:** the second reason comes from 1999 →

### 6 — The quote · *Insight*
**Idea:** HTML files are just telegrams — terse text the browser turns vivid.
**On screen:** the pull quote + attribution only — Neal Stephenson, *In the Beginning… Was the
Command Line* (1999).
**Say:** "Stephenson, 1999: however splendid the page, the HTML underneath is a telegram — terse
text. That observation matters more now than it did then."
**Hands off:** because guess what writes telegrams natively →

### 7 — A presentation is just HTML · *Insight payoff*
**Idea:** An LLM writes HTML natively — so MoonDeck has it author the finished deck directly.
**On screen:** transition slide — "A presentation is just HTML." · one line: the LLM authors the
finished deck, not a half-built draft you clean up.
**Say:** "A presentation is just HTML, and HTML is the one thing an LLM produces effortlessly.
So MoonDeck doesn't generate a half-built PowerPoint for you to fix — it writes the finished thing."
**Hands off:** and once a deck is text, it inherits everything code has →

### 8 — Decks get what code gets · *Insight payoff*
**Idea:** Text gives decks version history, one-click publishing, and real collaboration — firsts.
**On screen:** three cards — every version saved · a live website in one click · a team building
one deck together; punch: presentations never had this before.
**Say:** "Once a deck is text, it gets everything code gets. Every version saved and restorable.
Published to a live URL in one click. A whole team building the same deck. Presentations have
never had any of that."
**Hands off:** this isn't a demo reel — it's already shipped real keynotes →

### 9 — Real keynotes · *Proof*

**On screen:** two live deck thumbnails — MoonAgents launch keynote (Agentic Finance Summit) ·

**Say:** "OtherLife, our design agency, sent over brand assets — MoonDeck treats those as the
reference, so the agency's work compounds across every deck. I built the MoonAgents launch
keynote in an afternoon that way, and attendees asked if OtherLife had made it. Same for the CLI
talk in Barcelona and our Consensus deck — all MoonDeck."
**Hands off:** and when people see one →

### 11 — "Gamma, but for MoonPay" · *ROI*
**Idea:** Seat-based AI deck tools would cost $200K–$430K a year for ~400 people; MoonDeck is ~$0 marginal.
**On screen:** two bars — seat tool $200–430K/yr vs MoonDeck ~$0; note: enterprise seats run
